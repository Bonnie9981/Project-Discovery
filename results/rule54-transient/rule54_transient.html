The transient of a single cell under rule 54 on a cyclic lattice

Chi Hsia

Draft, November 2026

Abstract. Start elementary cellular automaton rule 54 from a single live cell on a ring of w cells. We prove that for every w ≥ 6 the orbit becomes periodic with period exactly 4 after T(w) steps, where T(w) = w − 3, (w − 6)/2, (w − 7)/2, (w − 4)/2 for w ≡ 1, 2, 3, 0 (mod 4), and that no earlier state recurs. The proof uses only the mirror symmetry of the orbit, the period-4 background pattern ("ether") of rule 54, and a short case check of local configurations modulo 4. All statements have been formalized and checked in the Lean 4 proof assistant (core library only).

1. Setting and result

Cells are indexed by i ∈ ℤ/w. Rule 54 updates every cell simultaneously by x′i = xi ⊕ (xi−1 ∨ xi+1): a cell flips exactly when at least one neighbour is alive. Let x0 have only cell 0 alive, and xt the state after t steps.

Theorem. Let w ≥ 6 and let T(w) be as above. Then xμ+4 = xμ holds exactly for μ ≥ T(w), and xT+p ≠ xT for p = 1, 2, 3. Hence the orbit enters a cycle of length exactly 4 at time T(w), and T(w) is the length of the transient.

(For w = 1, 2, 3, 5 the eventual period is 1 and the transients are 1, 2, 2, 5; for w = 4 the formula (w − 4)/2 = 0 also holds.) The values a(w) = T(w) start 1, 2, 2, 0, 5, 0, 0, 2, 6, 2, 2, 4, 10, 4, 4, 6, 14, …

2. The proof

Ether. Define E(t, d) (depending only on t mod 4 and d mod 4): for even t, E = 1 iff d ≡ t; for odd t, E = 1 iff d ≢ t + 1 (mod 4). Checking the 16 residue pairs shows that E is invariant under rule 54 on the infinite line: E(t+1, d) = E(t, d) ⊕ (E(t, d−1) ∨ E(t, d+1)). It is also symmetric about every even d.

Folding. The rule and the initial state are invariant under i ↦ −i, so every xt is mirror symmetric and is a function of the ring distance d = min(i, w − i) ∈ {0, …, m}, m = ⌊w/2⌋. On this segment rule 54 acts with reflecting ends: the lower neighbour of d = 0 is 1, and the upper neighbour of d = m is m − 1 if w is even and m itself if w is odd. From now on we work on the segment.

Light cone. By induction on t, as long as t + 2 ≤ m, cell d at time t is alive iff d ≤ t and E(t, d). (Inside the cone this is the ether identity; at the edge d = t, t + 1 it is a two-line check modulo 4; outside everything is dead.)

Case w ≡ 0 (mod 4). At t = T = (w − 4)/2 the cells outside the cone are exactly the three cells that the ether leaves dead at that time, so the whole ring is ether, which has period 4. One step earlier, the cells outside the cone form a block of 5 dead cells, which never occurs in the ether; so no earlier state lies on the cycle.

Cases w ≡ 2, 3 (mod 4). Here m = T + 3 with T even. At time T the segment is the ether (the cells T + 1, T + 2, T + 3 are ether-dead), but the reflection at the centre does not respect the ether. One checks, for the 8 values of (T mod 4, k mod 4), that at time T + k the state is the ether except that the centre cell d = m is dead when k ≡ 1 (mod 4): a stationary defect. This description has period 4 in k. Before T, cell T + 1 is dead at time T − 1 (outside the cone) but alive at time T + 3, so the orbit is not yet periodic.

Case w ≡ 1 (mod 4). Here w = 2m + 1 with m even. At t = m − 2 the cone has filled the segment and the state is exactly the ether. From then on, with the shifted ether N(t, d) = E(t + 1, d + 3), we claim that for t ≥ m − 2 the state at time t is

  • E(t, d) if d + t < w (in front of the wall),
  • N(t, d) ⊕ δ if d + t ≥ w (behind the wall), where the defect δ = 1 exactly when (d = 0 and t ≡ 2) or (d = m and t ≡ m) (mod 4).

So a phase wall is born at the far side at time m + 1 and walks back to the seed at speed 1, reaching it at time w; behind it, each end of the segment carries a one-cell defect once every 4 steps (neither end is a mirror point of N). The claim is proved by checking one step of rule 54 on it. The cases are: the position of the cell (seed end, next to it, interior, next to the centre, centre) times the position relative to the wall (all three cells in front, the cell about to cross, the cell just crossed, all behind). In each case every value involved depends only on t mod 4 and d mod 4 (with d + t fixed modulo 4 at the wall), so the check is finite.

From t = w − 3 on, the description has period 4: for d ≥ 3 both t and t + 4 are behind the wall, and for d ≤ 2 the ether value at t agrees with the defect-corrected N value at t + 4 (three residue checks). The transient is not shorter: cell 0 is alive at time w − 4 (ether, t ≡ 1) and dead at time w (behind the wall, N value, no defect since w ≡ 1). Exact period 4 follows by comparing cells 5 and 6. The small widths w = 9, 13 were checked directly.

Period exactly 4. In every case the cycle is described by the ether (possibly shifted, possibly with defects away from a fixed cell), and comparing one suitable cell shows xT+p ≠ xT for p = 1, 2, 3. A cycle of length 1 or 2 would already give equality at p = 1 or 2.

3. Verification and AI use

The theorem is formalized in Lean 4 (v4.15.0, core library only, no sorry, standard axioms) in github.com/Bonnie9981/Project-Discovery: Discovery.Rule54Mod4.rule54_mod4_zero, Discovery.Rule54Mod23.rule54_mod4_two, Discovery.Rule54Mod23.rule54_mod4_three, Discovery.Rule54Mod1.rule54_mod4_one (the last uses the finite kernel check Discovery.Rule54.rule54_transient_period for w = 9, 13). Three independent simulations agree with the formula cell by cell up to w = 1200.

The conjecture was found, and the proofs, the Lean formalization and this note were drafted, with the help of a large language model (Claude, Anthropic) inside the Project Discovery research pipeline; independent AI critics and reviewers checked them, and the Lean kernel checked the formal proofs. The ether and phase-wall language is standard for rule 54 (e.g. Hanson and Crutchfield, Physica D 103, 1997); we do not claim the techniques are new.
