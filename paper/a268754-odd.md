# Proof of the odd-index formula of OEIS A268754

Chi Hsia, November 2026. Proof and Lean 4 formalization produced with an LLM (Claude) and machine-checked; see the acknowledgement at the end.

## Statement

Let y_t^(n) be rule 90 on a wire of n cells with dead cells beyond both ends (null boundary), started from a single live cell at the left end; write a state as a bit string, bit i = cell i+1. A268754(n) = a(n) is the least eventual period of this orbit (the definition used by the PROG and MATHEMATICA of the entry).

**Theorem.** For every n >= 1:
1. the wire eventually dies (all cells dead) if and only if n+1 is a power of 2;
2. if n+1 is not a power of 2, then a(2n+1) = 2 a(n);
3. if n+1 = 2^k, then a(n) = a(2n+1) = 1.

## Proof

**Parity.** In y_t every live cell has index of the same parity as t (the seed is at index 0, and each new cell is the XOR of its two neighbours, which have equal parity).

**Spreading.** For a state y of the n-wire let S(y) be the state of the (2n+1)-wire with bit 2i+1 equal to bit i of y and all even bits 0 (in cell numbering 1..2n+1: the n-wire sits on cells 2, 4, ..., 2n). Two steps of the long wire act on S(y) as one step of the short wire: after one step the odd bits are 0 and bit 2i is y_{i-1} + y_i; after the second step the even bits are 0 and bit 2i+1 is (y_{i-1} + y_i) + (y_i + y_{i+1}) = y_{i-1} + y_{i+1} (mod 2), with the boundary cells matching on both sides. One step of the long wire takes the seed to S(seed). By induction, y^(2n+1)_{2t+1} = S(y^(n)_t) for all t, and S is injective.

**Doubling.** Suppose the n-wire never dies. If the long wire had an odd eventual period p, then for large odd s the states at times s and s+p would be equal while their live cells lie on odd and even indices respectively, so both would be 0; but the state at time s is S(y) with y nonzero. Hence every eventual period of the long wire is even, 2q, and by the spreading identity the long wire is eventually 2q-periodic iff the short wire is eventually q-periodic (even times are one step after odd times). So the least eventual periods satisfy a(2n+1) = 2 a(n).

**Death.** If n is even, the update map is injective: from nstep(y) = 0 one reads off that the odd bits of y vanish (from the left end up) and the even bits vanish (from the right end down), so the orbit of the nonzero seed never reaches 0. For n = 1 the seed dies in one step. For n = 2r+1 with r >= 1, by the spreading identity the long wire dies iff the short one does. Strong induction on n gives part 1, and parts 2 and 3 follow (a dying orbit has eventual period 1).

## Formal verification

Lean 4 (core only, no Mathlib, no native_decide): `lean/Discovery/A268754Odd.lean`, theorems `a268754_odd` (if p = a(n) then 2p = a(2n+1)), its converse `a268754_odd_iff`, `exists_leastPer_iff` (a least eventual period exists for the n-wire iff it does for the (2n+1)-wire), `a268754_odd_exception` and `dies_iff`. The general fact that a least eventual period exists (pigeonhole on finitely many states) is not formalized. The update map `nstep` is literally the Python update of the entry. Continuous integration checks that the theorems use only the standard axioms. Numerical cross-check by two implementations (bitmask wire and mirrored ring) for n <= 40 and against the 54 DATA terms: `results/A268754/code/l06_a268754_odd.py`.
